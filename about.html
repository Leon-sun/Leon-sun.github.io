About

Leon Sun

Business analyst. Ten years of requirements, process, and data across four industries and two continents.

I work in the space between what a business says it wants and what a system can actually do. That has meant different job titles — market researcher, data analyst, product manager, data scientist — but the work underneath has been consistent: talk to the people doing the job, find the evidence, and turn both into something a team can build against.

I spent most of the last decade in Shanghai. Five and a half years of that at Tuhu, an automotive services platform now listed in Hong Kong, where I led requirements and analysis across search, growth, and marketing — and learned that the hardest part of a data product is rarely the data. Before that, financial services: building an automated home-valuation service that partner banks used inside mortgage and credit decisions, which is where I first had to write requirements that a regulator might read.

I moved to Ottawa in 2024 for a master's in Digital Transformation and Innovation. Since then I've built reporting and governance for Nokia's North American and European operations, and I'm currently at the University of Ottawa doing process mining on a decade of pediatric hospital data with CHEO — reconstructing how care actually flows, and simulating what would change if the policy did.

How I work

Evidence before opinion. Most disagreements about what to build are really disagreements about what's happening now. Event logs, query logs, and a few hours of observation settle more arguments than a workshop does.

Definitions are deliverables. If three teams compute "active customer" three ways, no dashboard will save you. I write the definitions down and get them signed off, early.

Make it measurable before it's built. A requirement without a success metric is a preference. I'd rather agree on how we'll know it worked while the change is still cheap to argue about.
