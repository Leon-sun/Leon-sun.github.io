Nokia · 2025 · Reporting, data quality, governance

One set of numbers for two continents

Operations teams in North America and Europe monitoring the same programme with different numbers. Before any dashboard could be useful, the numbers had to be made to agree.

Company
Nokia — Ottawa, ON
Role
Data Reporting / Business Analyst
Delivered
8 Power BI dashboards & KPI scorecards; a Power Apps tool used across two organizations
Stack
Power BI, Power Apps, Power Automate, Dataverse, SQL

The situation

Operations stakeholders across two regions needed performance monitoring and a basis for allocating resources. They had data in several enterprise systems and no agreement on which system was authoritative for which measure — so regional reviews spent their first twenty minutes reconciling figures instead of acting on them.

What I did

  • Gathered reporting requirements from operations stakeholders in North America and Europe, working through the differences in how each region ran the same process.
  • Traced each metric to a source system, reconciled the discrepancies, and wrote standardized definitions with a named owner per metric.
  • Established data-quality and governance standards, including the checks that had to pass before a dashboard refresh was considered trustworthy.
  • Delivered eight Power BI dashboards and KPI scorecards for performance monitoring and resource allocation.
  • Translated a separate operational need into a Power Apps solution — with Power Automate and Dataverse — connecting two organizations and several hundred users to trace and analyze quality issues.

Result

Reviews started from an agreed set of figures. The documentation mattered as much as the dashboards: when a number is questioned six months later, the definition and its lineage are what let you answer instead of re-investigating.

What I'd take from it

Most reporting requests arrive as “we need a dashboard.” A useful share of them are actually requests to settle a definitional argument, and building the dashboard first just gives the argument a nicer interface.
